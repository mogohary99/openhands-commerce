# Demo Storyboard — under 3 minutes

**Format:** screen recording of the hosted app driven **keyboard-only with VoiceOver (or NVDA) audible**, captions burned in, presenter voice-over. No copyrighted music. Every product is labelled "fictional — OpenHands Demo Store, PayPal sandbox". The payment is a **real PayPal sandbox transaction** (no real money) — the receipt shows the real sandbox order and capture IDs, and we cut to the PayPal sandbox merchant dashboard to prove it.

| Time | Scene | On screen / audio | Criterion served |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Hook** | Black screen, screen-reader voice reading a real-world inaccessible checkout at speed. Caption: "60% of people would stop using an AI shopping agent after one mistake." (ACI 2026) "For a blind shopper, the mistake is often invisible." | Impact |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Who & what** | Presenter: "OpenHands Commerce is an accessible shopping companion that checks the thing before you pay for it." Show settings: text size, simple language, reduced motion; voice is optional. | Presentation, Design |
| 0:30–0:50 | **1. Need + budget** | User types (or pastes from AAC): "My phone uses USB-C. I need a charger with a cable, forty-five dollars all in." "Shopping for" card appears: Chargers · USB-C · $45.00 all-in. Screen reader: "Reply ready: 3 chargers found." | Design, Tech (AI extraction) |
| 0:50–1:15 | **2–3. Search + evidence** | Three cards; reasons cite listing facts: "Raised dot marks the port side — from the product listing." Reviews are labelled as customer reviews. Quick comparison table. | Tech, Innovation |
| 1:15–1:30 | **4. Select** | User picks "VoltLine 30W + 1 m cable" — and, like many of us, chooses the variant at the bottom of the list: **USB-C to Lightning**. | Story |
| 1:30–1:55 | **5–6. THE MOMENT** | Review screen. Screen reader immediately announces (alert): **"The cable will not fit your phone. This bundle includes a Lightning cable, but you said your phone uses USB-C."** User presses Approve anyway → nothing is charged; focus jumps to **"Switch to the USB-C cable bundle (same price)"**. Enter. "Your order now matches. Total $40.62 — within your $45.00 budget." | **Innovation, Impact, Design** |
| 1:55–2:05 | Brief tech overlay | Diagram: "AI explains. Server decides. You approve." No payment tools for the model; totals computed on the server; approval bound to this exact quote. | Tech |
| 2:05–2:30 | **7–8. Approve + PayPal** | "Approve and pay $40.62 with PayPal" → announced "Opening PayPal in a new window" → sandbox buyer logs in, pays → returns; focus on "Order confirmed". | Tech, Presentation |
| 2:30–2:45 | **9. Verified receipt** | Accessible receipt: items, shipping, tax, total, PayPal order + capture IDs, "Verified with PayPal: payment completed". Quick cut: same order in the PayPal sandbox business dashboard. | Tech (proof) |
| 2:45–2:58 | **Recovery + scale** | 3-second montage: popup closed → "Nothing was charged. Your cart is saved." · simulated decline → "Try another payment method." Close: "Any PayPal merchant can plug in their catalog. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA, tested with [only what actually happened]." | Impact, Presentation |

**Most memorable differentiating moment:** 1:30–1:55 — the assistant says, out loud, **"The cable will not fit your phone"**, refuses to let the purchase go ahead, and fixes it in one keystroke. It's concrete, it's the kind of error a sighted shopper catches by glance and a blind shopper only catches on delivery, and it shows "the AI explains, the server decides, the person approves" in a single beat.

**Backup plan for the recording:** record the PayPal segment separately and splice only if live capture fails, keeping the real sandbox order ID on screen; never use a mocked success screen.

**Accessibility of the video itself:** accurate captions (not auto-only), audio description of visual-only moments in the voice-over, transcript in the repo.
